# Heat training (core-temperature sensor)

For a cyclist with a core-temperature sensor (CORE or similar). A heat session is an ordinary easy endurance ride in a
hot room: it adds no length and no load to the plan, and the ride keeps its endurance targets. Numbers mirror
`assets/rules.json` `heat`.

Citations such as (E27) point at rows of `evidence.md`. The table at the end says, rule by rule, what a paper
supports and what is a coach heuristic or a conservative safety rule. The stop rules are safety rules: no paper
is needed to keep them, and none is a reason to relax them.

## Who and when

- Only a cyclist who has the sensor, and only on an easy endurance ride of **50–90 minutes** that is not outdoor-only
  (indoor first). Never a recovery spin, a key, a long ride, a test or a race.
- Never the day before a key, long, test or race day, never the day after a race, and never in the **7 days** before an
  A race.
- No block for a plan that starts without a base under it (comeback, off-season, start running).

## A block, then maintenance

Adaptation needs consecutive exposure, so heat comes as a block:

- **Initial block:** the first **2** load weeks of general or base from the plan's **second** load week (never week 0,
  week 1, a recovery or transition week), up to **4** sessions a week, consecutive days allowed. It needs at least
  **5** sessions; if two weeks cannot hold them, extend to **3** weeks, and if three still cannot, skip the block.
- **Maintenance:** after the block, **one a week** in general, base, build and peak; none in recovery, transition,
  taper or race week. Never on consecutive days.
- Twelve days without heat lose most of the adaptation (about 95 % down to about 42 %); re-acclimation takes about four
  sessions instead of ten.
- A pre-race heat block (10 sessions in 14 days ending 3–6 days before a hot race) is a real protocol but needs the
  event to be known as hot; not planned yet.

## The session

- **Setup:** indoors, no fan, one extra non-breathable layer, a heater or a closed room, bottles ready.
- **Warm-up:** 15 minutes easy until core reaches **38.5 °C**.
- **Zone:** hold **38.5–39.0 °C** for **40 minutes** at the same effort; at **39.0 °C** ease off or open a window.
- **Limit:** no more than **75 minutes** in the heat; then fan on and finish easy.
- **Stop:** at **39.5 °C**, or at once if dizzy, sick, chilled or with a headache.
- **Effort:** endurance power or RPE 3–4; heart rate drifts to about **75–80 % of max** (lower in a hotter, more humid
  room). That reads as Z3 in the cycling heart-rate bands of `testing-and-zones.md`; the ride is still endurance by
  power, and the heat accounts for the difference.
- **Drink:** to sweat rate, usually **1–2 litres an hour**, with a strong electrolyte mix; weigh before and after and
  replace one and a half times the loss over the next hours.
- **Cool-down:** 10 minutes easy with the fan on.
- **Skip it** when ill, after a bad night, or the day after another heat session outside a block. A block is extra
  load: keep the rest of the week easy.

## Evidence and standing of each rule

Every number in this guide is listed here. A number is called **Supported** only when a paper read in full
gives it; a paper known only from its abstract is named as such and never carries a number on its own. Anything
else is a coach heuristic or a conservative safety rule.

| Rule in this guide | Standing | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Heat adaptation needs repeated exposure, so it comes as a block | **Supported** | Repeated exercise-heat exposure over 1–2 weeks is the consensus recommendation (E27). A meta-analysis found longer regimens more effective and advises starting at least 14 days before a hot event (E29). |
| At least 5 sessions in the block | **Supported** | Five or more days were enough to adapt heart rate and core temperature (E30). |
| Up to 4 sessions a week over 2–3 weeks, consecutive days allowed | **Supported in part** | Consecutive days are preferable, and exposure every second or third day still produces adaptation (E29). It produces less: ten sessions spread over three weeks, which is close to this guide's block, adapted riders less than daily sessions did (E30). So put the block's sessions on consecutive days where the week allows. The exact pattern is this guide's. |
| Sessions of 50–90 min | **Supported in part** | The consensus asks for at least 60 min a day (E27); the trials reviewed used 60–120 min (E30), and the controlled-temperature trial used 90 min (E31). The 50-min floor is a coach heuristic. |
| Hold core temperature at 38.5 °C | **Supported** | Controlled hyperthermia to at least 38.5 °C is one of the consensus methods (E27). In a trial, sessions that held 38.5 °C gave the same adaptation as fixed-intensity sessions with less exercise (E31). A review known here only from its abstract reaches the same view (E28). |
| 40 min in the zone | **Supported in part** | In the trial, the controlled-temperature groups spent about 32–49 min of each 90-min session at or above 38.5 °C (E31). No paper sets a minimum; 40 min is this guide's choice inside that range. |
| No more than 75 min in the heat | **Conservative safety rule** | Shorter than the 90-min sessions studied (E31). A cap for unsupervised sessions, not a finding. |
| Ease off at 39.0 °C, stop at 39.5 °C | **Conservative safety rule** | The consensus gives no stop temperature (E27). The trial held 39.0 °C as a target only in its second week and under supervision, and its laboratory ended a test at 39.7 °C (E31). This guide sets both limits lower on purpose, because the sessions are at home, alone, with a consumer sensor. Never raise either. |
| Stop at once if dizzy, sick, chilled or with a headache | **Conservative safety rule** | Standard signs of heat illness. Symptoms outrank the sensor. |
| One maintenance session a week after the block | **Coach heuristic, and less often than was tested** | A trial known here only from its abstract kept the adaptations with one exposure every fifth day (E32). Exposures seven days apart did not produce adaptation in the studies reviewed (E29, E30). Once a week may therefore be too little to hold the adaptation; see the note on re-acclimation below. |
| "Twelve days without heat lose most of the adaptation (about 95 % down to about 42 %)" | **Unverified, and probably too pessimistic** | No source was found for these figures. The meta-analysis puts the loss at about 2.5 % of the heart-rate and core-temperature adaptation per day without heat, and reports a study in which about 30 % of the heart-rate adaptation was lost after twelve days (E30). The consensus says most benefits last 2–4 weeks (E27). |
| Re-acclimation takes about four sessions instead of ten | **Supported in part** | Re-acclimation is much faster than the first acclimation: two days restored heart rate after twelve days without heat and four days after twenty-six, and the authors suggest 4–5 days of re-acclimation before an event (E30). |
| Never the day before a key, long, test or race day; never in the 7 days before an A race; none in a taper | **Coach heuristic** | Scheduling caution to protect the key sessions and the taper. It is not what the heat literature advises for a hot race, which is to re-acclimate in the days before it (E30); this guide does not plan heat for a specific race. |
| Pre-race block of 10 sessions in 14 days, ending 3–6 days before a hot race | **Unverified** | In line with starting at least 14 days out (E29) and the 1–2 weeks of the consensus (E27), but the exact shape has no source here, and the guide does not plan it. |
| No fan, an extra layer, a closed room | **Coach heuristic** | A way to raise core temperature at endurance power. The studies used heat chambers at about 40 °C (E31). |
| Heart rate drifts to about 75–80 % of max | **Coach heuristic** | An expectation, not a target and not a limit. |
| Drink 1–2 litres an hour with electrolytes | **Supported** | Sweat rates of 1.0–1.5 L/h are common in the heat and some people exceed 2.5 L/h; sodium of 0.5–0.7 g per litre is advised for exercise over an hour (E27). Drink to the measured sweat loss and not beyond it: overdrinking carries its own risk (E27). |
| Replace one and a half times the weight lost | **Supported** | 150 % of the body-mass loss, with electrolytes, when rapid replacement is needed; 100–120 % is more realistic (E27). |
| Only with a core-temperature sensor | **Conservative safety rule** | The target is a core temperature, so it cannot be followed safely by feel. |

What the evidence does not cover: the adaptation in athletes over 60, in people with a heart condition or on
medication that affects sweating or blood pressure, and the accuracy of a consumer sensor against the rectal or
ingested thermometers the studies used. Anyone in the first two groups asks a physician before any heat session.
