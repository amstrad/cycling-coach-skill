# Fuelling and recovery cues

The skill does not write diet plans. It adds short, standard cues to sessions so key work is not failed for lack of fuel. Never prescribe weight loss, calorie targets, or fasted high-intensity work.

## Carbohydrate during sessions

| Session | Carbohydrate | Fluid |
|---|---|---|
| < 60 min easy | none needed | to thirst |
| < 60 min HI or threshold | optional; start fed | 500 ml/h |
| 60–90 min | 30–60 g/h | 500–750 ml/h |
| 90 min–3 h | 60–90 g/h | 500–750 ml/h, with sodium in heat |
| > 3 h, races, marathon | 80–90 g/h (up to 120 g/h only if gut-trained), glucose + fructose mixes above 60 g/h | 500–1000 ml/h, 300–600 mg sodium/l in heat |

Marathon and long-event plans include gut training: practise the race-day intake on every long run or ride in the final 8 weeks.

## Around key sessions

- Eat a carbohydrate-based meal 2–4 h before HI or threshold sessions, or a small snack 30–60 min before.
- After key sessions and long days: carbohydrate plus 20–40 g protein within about an hour, especially when the next session is within 24 h.
- Daily protein around 1.6 g/kg; older athletes (50+) benefit from 0.4 g/kg per meal spread across meals.
- Low-carbohydrate or fasted riding is optional and only for easy Z2 under 90 min, never before or during HI work, never for beginners.

## Sleep and recovery

- 7–9 h is the target; under 6 h counts as a readiness flag (`planning-rules.md`, rule 12).
- The hardest session of the week should not follow the shortest night when the calendar allows a swap.

## Red flags to surface (refer to a professional)

Persistent fatigue, frequent illness, stalled or falling performance with rising effort, lost or irregular menstrual cycles, stress fractures, rapid weight change, or a stated restrictive eating pattern. These can signal low energy availability (RED-S). Flag gently, keep the week easy, and recommend a physician or sports dietitian.
