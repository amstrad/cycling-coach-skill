# Coaching model — the principles

These are the general rules for arranging the week. Priorities come from the derived power profile (`power-profiles.md`), the athlete's level (`athlete-levels.md`), the phase of the season (`periodization.md`), and the objective. FTP, weight, goal W/kg, and objective come from `athlete-profile.json` and the Strava or Garmin sync. For running, read this together with `running-model.md`.

## Find the limiter

Read the limiter from the data, not from a template. A strong top end with modest sustained power means FTP is the limiter (sub-threshold, threshold, aerobic ceiling). Strong sustained power with a weak top end means repeatability and aerobic ceiling are the limiter (aerobic ceiling plus anaerobic work). Use `power-profiles.md`. Durability (power after 2–3 h) is a third limiter: compare the best 20-min power in the last hour of long rides with the fresh 20-min best; a drop > 10 % calls for long rides with late tempo, not more intervals.

## Shape of the week

- Easy-heavy distribution: at most `targetedSessionCap` targeted key sessions; everything else genuinely easy (≤ 70 % FTP, RPE ≤ 3). The time-in-zone targets by volume are in `planning-rules.md`.
- `socialDays` are ridden at the group's pace with no imposed structure. Group riding is also specific practice for bunch objectives.
- If a social ride ran hard, count it as a quality day and soften the next midweek session. Never stack three hard days.
- Order quality by freshness: the highest-intensity session first after the rest day, the threshold-type session later, the long ride at the weekend.
- Guide by power (or pace), not heart rate. Put targets first and allow the rider to ease off by feel; heart rate and RPE are the sanity check, and a mismatch of RPE +2 against the expected effort is a readiness signal.
- Every hard constraint in `planning-rules.md` outranks a session preference. If a constraint fails, move the session, then downgrade it to Z2, then drop it.

## Four-week block

Default to three build weeks plus one lighter week unless the rider's load history argues otherwise:

- **Weeks 1–3:** progressive overload — move the key sessions up one progression step each week (see the table in `interval-catalog.md`), only when the push test in `planning-rules.md` passes.
- **Week 4:** recovery and test — one targeted midweek session at reduced reps, a shorter long ride, and, when appropriate, a segment effort or time trial at the end of the week to measure adaptation.

For riders 50+, beginners, and anyone who failed the push test two weeks running, switch to a 2-build + 1-recovery rhythm. Phase emphasis (base, build, peak, taper) comes from `periodization.md`.

## Recovery spacing by age

Layer these rules on top of the easy-heavy structure. Read `age` from the profile; the fuller table is in `planning-rules.md`.

- **Under 40:** one easy or rest day between hard sessions is enough.
- **40–49:** never back-to-back hard days; two easy days between two high-intensity sessions; cut HI reps by about 20 %.
- **50+:** never back-to-back hard days, and two easy or rest days after every high-intensity session or race (a threshold-type session may follow one easy day). From 60, two easy days follow every hard day; cut weekly volume before cutting intensity; keep one HI touch a week; strength twice a week; take a recovery week every 2–3 weeks instead of every 4.

## Level

- **Beginner:** frequency, then duration, then intensity. One key session (sub-threshold or tempo); no HI work in the first 8 consistent weeks.
- **Intermediate:** the standard model as written here.
- **Advanced:** may run a short HI block (3 HI sessions in one week, at most twice a season) and add quality inside long rides.

## Standard session shapes

- Sub-threshold blocks: 2 × 20 min at ~90 % FTP, 5 min easy between. Progress to 2 × 25.
- Threshold: 2 × 15 min at 95–100 %. Progress to 2 × 20 or 3 × 15.
- Oscillating threshold: 3 sets of 8 min, alternating 3 min at 95 % and 1 min at 105 %, with 5 min easy between sets.
- Aerobic ceiling: 5 × 4 min at ~115 % FTP, 4 min easy between, cadence 95+; or 30/15s micro-intervals, 3 × 13 reps.
- Short-sharp: 40/20s or 30/30s, 2–3 sets of 8–10 reps (for steady riders and criterium objectives).
- Tempo: 2 × 30 min or 1 × 60 min at 76–85 %, inside a longer ride (durability and base).
- Aerobic base: 60–180 min at 60–70 % FTP, genuinely easy (85–95 rpm).
- Low-cadence force: 3–5 × 6–8 min at 70–80 % FTP and 55–65 rpm, seated. Builds force without a large cardiovascular cost; useful before climbing blocks.
- Torque starts: 6–10 × 10–12 s maximal seated efforts from a rolling start in a big gear, near-full recovery between reps. Very low volume, high neuromuscular value.

## What the week must never do

Two HI days in a row; three hard days in a row; heavy gym the day before intervals; a progression step the rider did not earn; volume and intensity raised together; a skipped deload; a benchmark while fatigued or ill. The full checklist is in `planning-rules.md`.
